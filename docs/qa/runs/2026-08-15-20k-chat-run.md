# 20 000-message chat, sent through the API

Date: 2026-08-15. Devices: the owner's two simulators (iPhone 17 dev as sender,
iPhone 17 Pro Max as receiver). Stand: `wrangler dev` on :8787, APNs mock on :9871.

The goal was a chat large enough to stress the real send path: messages go
through `engine.enqueue`, so they are encrypted, queued in the outbox, sent to
the server and delivered over the socket exactly as a person's messages would
be. The DEBUG-only seeding section in the chat profile screen drives it.

## Result

29 080 messages in the chat, identical on both devices: seq 1…29 080, no
duplicate seqs, no gaps, no unreadable messages, nothing left in the decrypt
queue. Text of the last message matches on both sides.

The chat opens on the newest message and pages upwards through local history
without stalling. A flick scrolls about 50 messages and the client settles back
to idle CPU afterwards.

## What the run exposed

**Sending slowed down as history grew.** 12 messages/s at 1.7k messages in the
chat, 5/s at 4.7k, 4/s at 7k, with the sending client at 158% CPU against 10%
for the server. The feed window had no ceiling: its floor was computed once on
entering the chat and never rose, so every arriving message widened the window
permanently, and one insert re-read, re-decoded and re-diffed everything in it
(5475 rows at the time of the measurement). Fixed in 4806bf3; the window now
slides against a capacity while the reader is at the newest message and stays
put while they are reading older ones. After the fix: 25–31 messages/s at 10k
history, six times faster than before the fix at half the history size.

**The feed drifted under incoming traffic.** The unread marker moved around the
screen although its anchor never changed: the whole list was shifting. The list
is flipped, so an insert lands in item 0 and pushes everything above it under an
unchanged contentOffset. Updates now record the topmost visible item and restore
its screen position afterwards. Verified under a live burst of ~25 messages/s:
316 messages arrived over four seconds (marker count 2157 → 2473) with the list
and the marker staying exactly where they were.

**Receiving is four times slower than sending.** The receiver ingested 6–12
messages/s at 183% CPU while the sender sustained 24–31/s, and the gap grew to
8735 messages by the time sending finished, taking another 25 minutes to drain.
Nothing was lost and catch-up completed cleanly, but at that rate a burst is
visible to a person as minutes of lag. Tracked separately.

**The APNs mock took down the host.** It spawns one `xcrun simctl push` process
per notification with no bound; roughly 1100 of them accumulated, exhausted the
host's IOSurface client pool, and for about half an hour every CoreSimulator
client on the machine — `simctl`, `xcodebuild`, screenshots — failed at startup.
Tracked separately.

## Note on the notification observations from this run

The banners seen during the run showed the fallback body "Новое сообщение", no
sender avatar, and a system summary "You have 54 new Msngr notifications". None
of that is a product defect: on a simulator `xcrun simctl push` never invokes the
notification service extension at all, so the payload is presented unprocessed
(control experiment in `docs/research/nse-simulator-experiment.md`). The real
ceiling on extension invocations can only be measured on hardware against live
APNs.
