# Voice messages, both sides

Five lines of the roadmap said done and none of them had been used: the 0.3 s
cut, the accidental touch, slide-to-cancel with the lock, the playback speed,
and playback carrying on into another chat. This run put a finger on each of
them on two devices and rewrote what did not hold.

Run date: 2026-08-18, finished on the 19th.

## Stand

Own `wrangler dev` on :8871 with `--persist-to .claude/stand-voice` outside the
repository, its APNs host pointed at :9899, the three D1 migrations applied into
it. The shared stand on :8787 was not touched.

Two own simulators, iPhone 17, iOS 26.5, microphone granted with `xcrun simctl
privacy <udid> grant microphone ai.enface.Msngr`, deleted after the run:

- `voice-a` (3CE1F846) as `voicea2` — the sending side,
- `voice-b` (A4AE9439) as `voiceb2` — the receiving side.

Build from the working tree of `run-voice`, every build through
`scripts/build-slot.py`. The gate ran on `voice-a` against the shared stand with
the app removed first, so nothing of this run's database was carried into it:
`make check DEV_UDID=3CE1F846…` green, no fresh crashes.

## What the gesture did before

Reading the way in found three things a screenshot would never have shown.

The microphone is asked for asynchronously. A tap shorter than the answer left
the "starting" flag set, the answer came back after the finger was gone, and the
recorder started with nobody holding it — a take that ends only when the screen
does. A take cancelled by the slide could be started again by the same finger
still moving over the button, and that second take was sent on release. And the
audio session was kept after `stop()`, so the red bar stayed up and other apps
stayed ducked long after the take was over.

The rules live in `RecordingGesture` now, a value type with no view attached,
and `RecordingGestureTests` holds fourteen of them: the tap that ends before the
permission answer, the refusal, the finger that goes on moving after a cancel,
the lock outliving the finger, the interruptions.

## The run

Sending side, `voice-a`:

| What | Fact |
|------|------|
| a tap on the microphone | no recording bar, composer back, feed unchanged (`testA`, 29 s) |
| slide left past 110 pt | bar down, nothing sent, the finger moving on starts nothing (`testB`, 29 s) |
| slide up past 70 pt, finger off | recording carries on, with a send and a cancel of its own (`testC`, 25 s) |
| the send button of a locked take | the message lands in the feed (`testD`, 30 s) |
| leaving the chat mid-take | nothing sent, nothing left running (`testE`, 31 s) |
| the app sent to the background mid-take | the same (`testE1`, 27 s) |
| playback | the position walks the wave, the button steps ×1 → ×1,5 → ×2 (`testF`, 24 s) |
| three seconds at ×1 against three at ×2 on a 20 s take | the second sample more than half as long again as the first (`testF1`, 51 s) |
| walking out to the list and back mid-message | still playing, position further along (`testG`, 58 s) |
| a tap at 80% of the wave, then at 15% | starts there, then moves back (`testH`, 34 s) |

Receiving side, `voice-b`, on the takes the peer sent:

| What | Fact |
|------|------|
| the message that arrived | plays, the position moves (`testF`, 30 s) |
| the speed chosen on one message | still chosen when another starts (`testI`, 43 s) |
| walking out to the list and back | still playing (`testJ`, 55 s) |

The material: AAC, 1 channel, 24000 Hz. The two takes the peer fetched and
played are 84415 bytes for 8.445 s and 84011 bytes for 12.584 s, the same sizes
they have on the sender.

The first message from someone new arrives as a request, and its content stays
closed until it is accepted; that is what the receiving device sees before
anything can be played at all.

## The 0.3 s cut

Live, an accidental tap never reaches the recorder: the finger is gone before
the permission answer and the gesture drops the take. The cut itself is a named
rule now — `VoiceRecorder.isAccidental` — because a test that tried to record
less than 0.3 s recorded 0.35 and read as a defect of the product rather than of
its own timing. `VoiceRecorderTests` checks the rule exactly and then asks the
recorder for what it can prove on any machine: the shortest take it can make
either comes back over the cut or is dropped with its file. Those tests record
for real, so on a simulator without the microphone they step aside instead of
passing quietly.

## Defects found and fixed

- An accidental touch could leave a recorder running (the permission race).
- A cancelled take could be started again by the same finger and sent.
- A take was not dropped when the screen went away, the app left the foreground
  or the audio session was interrupted: it ran on and was sent on release.
- The audio session was held after every take.
- Speed had no button at all. It is on the message being played now, and the
  rate is applied after `play()` — set before it, `prepareToPlay` eats it and
  every message comes out at ×1.
- Every bubble polled the player on a timer of its own. One `@Published`
  `VoicePlayback` replaced them, which is also what lets a bubble reused while
  another chat's message is playing show the right position at once.
- The gallery started a voice message under the server's `msgId` while the feed
  knows it by the local row id, so a message played from the gallery did not
  show as playing in the feed. Both use the row id now.

## Seen once, not explained

On the shared stand, a tap on the row of a chat that had appeared a second
earlier did not open it — twice in one run of thirteen tests, on a list of one
row, with the second test opening the same chat straight away. Nothing in the
feed or the player is involved; it looks like the touch landing while the list
is still settling. The run gives the row a second touch before failing, so the
next time this happens the log will say how often. Not reproduced on demand and
not counted as a defect yet.

## What a simulator could not answer

An incoming call cannot be raised on a simulator. The closest thing that was run
is the app leaving the foreground, which takes the same path
(`RecordingGesture.interrupted()`); the audio-session branch of it is covered by
the unit test alone.

## The host

Half the wall-clock of this run went to a machine that could not be measured on.
With the disk at 98% and the load average above 400 at almost no CPU, a test
that takes 60 s took 1633 s, the app under test missed its own display link
ticks, and the position on the wave stayed at zero — which reads exactly like a
player that does not play. Nothing was concluded from those runs. The automatic
sweep that would have taken the disk back had been dead for a day:
`scripts/tidy.py` was falling over `int(inf)` on a simulator our app had never
run on, and `scripts/disk.py` over a file name that is not valid UTF-8 in the
output of `lsof`. Both are one line each and are on main now.
