# Group calls

Owner's request, 2026-09-02: group calls, not on Cloudflare Calls. The block
CALLS-ROADMAP.md has been pointing at: a call of a group chat, a call that
grows past three, everyone hearing and seeing everyone, with the media never
readable by a server.

## What is there now

1:1 audio and video run peer to peer over WebRTC: the signaling is E2EE
`call` service content on the message path, the SDP inside the envelope
authenticates the endpoints, DTLS-SRTP carries the media, coturn on adad
relays the paths STUN cannot cross. Pulling a third person into a 1:1 call
builds a short-lived mesh: every pair on its own transport, the callId as the
ticket. The mesh is capped at two extra legs (`extras.count < 2`), so a fourth
person can be pulled in and a fifth is ignored by `CallManager.invite`
without a word to the user (logged in `docs/qa/defects.md`). Growing the mesh
further is the wrong fight: each phone uploads its stream once per peer.

## The choice

Three ways to get past the mesh without Cloudflare Calls were weighed:

1. LiveKit, self-hosted on adad, with LiveKit's frame encryption. Chosen.
   One container, an official Swift SDK, room signaling done. The frame
   cryptor encrypts every audio and video frame with a key the SFU never
   holds, so the server forwards ciphertext and the E2EE promise of the
   messenger survives the group call. The key travels the way every secret
   of a call already travels: inside our E2EE signaling.
2. LiveKit without frame encryption. A day shorter, and the SFU decrypts
   every group call. Rejected: the hole would have to be closed later anyway.
3. mediasoup on adad. Node, no iOS client (the mediasoup-client protocol
   would be written by hand over plain WebRTC), frame encryption also by
   hand. Several times the work for the same result.

The WebRTC binary changes with the choice: the LiveKit SDK ships its own
build (`livekit/webrtc-xcframework`, pinned by the SDK), and two frameworks
cannot both provide the `WebRTC` module. The 1:1 transport moves onto that
build. It uses only the standard peer-connection classes, which both builds
share; the 1:1 path itself does not change.

## Architecture

### The SFU on adad

`livekit-server` in docker with host networking, run by systemd
(`msngr-livekit.service`), config and state in `/root/msngr/livekit/`.
Signaling on :7880 reaches the clients only through the cloudflared tunnel
as `sfu.a-kuz.online` (a second ingress rule in `msngr.yml`). Media goes
straight to the host's public address: UDP 50000–60000 and the TCP fallback
7881, with `use_external_ip: true`. The relay for hard NATs is the coturn
already on the host, handed to LiveKit as `turn_servers`. One API key pair
(`msngr` / a generated secret) lives in the LiveKit config and in the
Worker's `.dev.vars`. Rooms are created on join and closed by the SFU a
minute after the last participant leaves.

### The Worker: a room ticket

`POST /api/calls/room {callId, chatId}` → `{url, token}`. The Worker checks
the caller is a member of `chatId` and mints a LiveKit access token (HS256
JWT: `iss` the API key, `sub` the userId, `video: {room: callId, roomJoin,
canPublish, canSubscribe}`, one hour). The room name is the callId, which
lives only inside E2EE content, so knowing it is the ticket exactly as it
is for the mesh today. The token gates who may load the SFU at all; the
frame key gates who can hear anything.

### Signaling: the `room` invite

`CallSignal` gets a type `room`: `{type: "room", callId, key, video?,
members?}`. `key` is 32 random bytes, base64, generated by whoever opens
the room. A `room` signal rings like an `offer` does (same freshness window
of 60 s, same busy and privacy gate, same ringtone); accepting it does not
answer over signaling — the device fetches a ticket and joins the room, and
the others learn it is there from the SFU. `end(decline)` goes back as
today. There is no `answer`, no `ice`, no renegotiation inside a room: the
SFU owns the transport.

The signal is sent once into the chat the call belongs to. In a group chat
that is one message to the whole group through the sender key, and every
member rings; in a 1:1 chat it is the upgrade of a live call (below).

### The live card, in rooms

`CallLive` gains `key`. The card is written into the chat the call belongs
to by whoever opened the room, and a tap on it fetches a ticket and joins:
the card carries everything a late joiner needs. Anyone in the room may
edit the card, and the last one out closes it — `applyEdit` does not bind
an edit to the row's author, and the card is service content of the same
chat, so the client accepts the edit from any member. The writer does not
have to stay for the card to stay true.

### The client: a second kind of call

`CallManager` keeps one call per device and gains a room mode beside the
peer mode. Media in a room is behind a new seam, `CallRoomSession`
(`join(url, token, key, video)`, `setMuted`, `setVideo`, `leave`, an event
stream of participants joining, leaving, speaking, muting, turning video on
and off), implemented in `MsngrCalls` over the LiveKit `Room` with
`E2EEOptions` on a shared-key `BaseKeyProvider`. `CallState` gains
`participants: [Participant]` (userId, speaking, muted, video), and the
phase machine is the same one: dialing while nobody else has joined,
connecting while the SFU negotiates, active once media flows.

Entry points:

- the phone and video buttons in a group chat's header open a room and send
  the `room` invite to the group;
- «add a person» on a 1:1 call opens a room, sends the `room` invite with the
  same callId to the peer over their chat — the peer joins in place and both
  drop the peer-to-peer transport once the room carries their media — and to
  the invited person over their direct chat, with the invited-by row as
  today;
- a tap on a live card joins.

The mesh goes: `PeerLink`, `extras`, the leg promotion on the primary's
exit. Call waiting and hold stay as they are for the 1:1 leg; a room call
is left, not held.

### The screen

A room shows a grid of tiles instead of the single avatar: two columns,
each tile the participant's video when their camera is on and their avatar
and name when it is not, a ring while they speak, a muted glyph when their
microphone is off. The self tile sits in the grid. The controls are the
ones a call has: mute, camera, flip, add a person, leave. The floating tile
over the chats shows the count and the timer.

### The log

A room call leaves the live card as its record, closed with `endedAt` and
the final members; the calls list shows it with the group's name and the
duration, and a tap on a finished card of a group chat starts a new call in
that chat. No `callLog` is sent for a room call: nobody is the caller.

## Failure handling

- A `room` invite the privacy gate refuses or that lands on a busy device
  is answered `end(busy)`, as an offer is.
- A ticket the Worker refuses, or a room the SFU will not join, ends the
  call as `failed` on the device that tried; the others are untouched.
- A participant whose socket to the SFU drops is reconnected by the SDK;
  the screen shows «Подключение…» meanwhile, and a reconnect that gives up
  ends the call as `failed`.
- A frame that arrives under a key this device does not hold is dropped by
  the cryptor. Everyone in a call holds the one key of that call; the key
  is not rotated when someone leaves, and this is written down as the
  limit of v1 (a left participant who kept the key and a valid ticket could
  rejoin within the ticket's hour; the ticket gate is the chat membership).

## Testing

- `CallManagerTests`: the room mode over a fake `CallRoomSession` — a
  group invite rings and joins, decline and busy, the 1:1 upgrade drops
  the peer transport once the room is up, the last one out closes the
  card, a fresh room signal older than 60 s does not ring.
- `CallSignalTests`: the `room` signal and the card's `key` round-trip.
- Server smoke: the ticket endpoint refuses a non-member and returns a JWT
  that verifies against the secret with the expected grants.
- The live run: `alfa`, `bravo`, `charlie` in `Standup` on three
  simulators plus a fourth fresh account pulled in from the call — everyone
  hears everyone, video on and off per tile, the card in the chat follows
  the room, the last one out closes it. The SFU's own log confirms it saw
  the room; the frame encryption is checked by joining the room with a
  ticket and no key and hearing nothing.
